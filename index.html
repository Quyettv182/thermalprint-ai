ThermalPrint AI is a software layer for desktop 3D printing that simulates per-layer thermal behavior, repairs broken meshes automatically, and optimizes G-code for speed, part strength, and surface quality.

The problem: prints fail thermally, and slicers can't see it coming

Warping, weak layer adhesion, heat creep, and surface defects are usually thermal problems. Today's slicers slice geometry — they never simulate how heat builds up and dissipates layer by layer, so users only discover the failure after hours of printing and wasted filament.

Three modules, one goal: fewer failed prints

ThermalPrint AI works as a standalone engine with post-processing adapters and a companion app for the slicers people already use — Bambu Studio, OrcaSlicer, PrusaSlicer, and Cura.

Module 1 · Assess

Thermal simulation & TQI heatmap

Per-layer thermal simulation over your G-code and material profile, visualized as a heatmap with a single Thermal Quality Index (TQI) score — red zones overheat, blue zones overcool, before a single gram of filament is spent.

Module 2 · Repair

Automatic mesh repair

Hole closing, flipped-normal correction, and non-manifold edge repair with a per-error report — and a clean refusal when a mesh cannot be saved, instead of a silently broken print.

Module 3 · Enhance

Dynamic G-code optimization

Flow–temperature compensation that raises nozzle temperature when volumetric flow spikes, adaptive cooling for overhangs and layer bonding, and arc fitting that shrinks files and smooths toolpaths.

How it works

No new slicer to learn. ThermalPrint AI plugs into the workflow you already have.

1 · Slice as usualExport G-code (or .gcode.3mf) from Bambu Studio, OrcaSlicer, PrusaSlicer, or Cura.
2 · AssessThe engine simulates thermal behavior per layer and returns a TQI heatmap plus a mesh-repair report.
3 · EnhanceParameters are rewritten — temperature, cooling, feedrate, arcs — for your goal: Max Speed, Max Strength, or High-Gloss.
4 · Print with confidenceSend the optimized file to the printer, with the risk already engineered out.
